The distribution is long-tailed to the right: a handful of futures go extremely well. Pay attention to the left edge instead. That is where you actually live if things go averagely, and it is the part of the picture optimism hides.

These are live: they recompute from your assumptions, splitting the runs by whether they finished above a million. Month 6 is the checkpoint that matters — by then the two populations barely overlap, so you learn whether this is working after six months of part-time effort rather than after two years.

What this does not know. It treats the sale of the business as instantaneous at your horizon; selling a small agency really takes 6–12 months, so the timing is optimistic even where the magnitude is honest. It contains no model of your motivation across two years of unglamorous outbound, no recession, no lawsuit, and no key contractor quitting in month ten.

Elasticities and conversion rates are judgement, not measurement. Run python3 run.py robust in the repository to see the price recommendation reverse if the elasticity guess is wrong — then measure your own with two price points over sixty days.

Full model, 63 tests and the written strategy live in the repo. This is a thinking tool. It is not a forecast, and it is not financial advice.
