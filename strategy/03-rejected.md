# What I considered and threw out

With the arithmetic, so you can check it rather than trust it. Every one of
these fails on your four constraints: under $5k, 20 hours a week, sales ability
only, 24 months.

## High-rate solo consulting

The obvious move for someone with a valuable skill. The arithmetic kills it:

- 20 hrs/week × 52 weeks × 2 years = **2,080 billable hours**, and that assumes
  100% utilisation, which nobody achieves. Realistically 60% → 1,250 hours.
- $1,000,000 after 30% tax = **$1,430,000 billed**.
- $1,430,000 ÷ 1,250 = **$1,144 per hour.**

That is top-tier expert-witness pricing and it requires deep domain expertise
you told me you do not have. Even at an excellent $300/hour you reach $375,000
over two years — a fine outcome, a quarter of the goal.

The deeper problem: consulting builds **no asset**. You cannot sell it, so you
forfeit the two-thirds of your probability mass that lives in the exit. Every
hour you stop working, the business stops earning.

**Verdict: arithmetically impossible.** This is the same business as the
recommended plan, minus the leverage that makes it work.

## E-commerce, dropshipping, Amazon FBA

- Inventory, ad testing and a functioning creative pipeline need **$20,000–50,000**
  before you learn anything reliable. You have under $5,000.
- Paid acquisition is a market where you bid against companies with better data
  and more capital, and your only edge would be creative — which is a skill you
  have, but one you would be renting to a margin structure of 5–15%.
- At a 10% net margin, $1M of profit needs **$10,000,000 in revenue** in
  24 months.

**Verdict: eliminated by capital.** The model's `starting_capital` ruin check
would fire in month two.

## Trading, crypto, leverage

$5,000 → $1,000,000 is a **200× return**. To do that inside 24 months requires
either sustained returns no retail trader achieves, or leverage that makes ruin
the modal outcome rather than the tail.

The structural issue is not that big wins are impossible — it is that the
strategy required to produce them is the same one that produces zero most of the
time, and you cannot re-enter after ruin because you have no second $5,000. Your
entire capital base is one draw.

**Verdict: eliminated by ruin risk.** Everything else on this list has a p10
outcome above $100,000. This one's p10 is zero.

## Courses, info products, an audience play

The economics are genuinely excellent — 90% margins, infinite scale, and a
saleable asset at the end. The blocker is timing.

- You have **no audience**. That was in the intake.
- Building one to the size that supports $1M in sales takes, realistically,
  **two to four years** of consistent output before the revenue curve bends.
- Your deadline is 24 months, so you would spend the entire window building the
  distribution and none of it monetising.

**Verdict: right business, wrong deadline.** If your horizon were 5–10 years
this would be at the top of the list rather than the bottom.

## Software, SaaS, micro-SaaS

Best economics available: high margins, low churn, 4–8× revenue multiples rather
than 2.6× SDE. And unavailable to you.

- You cannot build it (no technical skill).
- You cannot hire someone to build it (a competent contract developer for
  12 months is $80,000–150,000, against under $5,000).
- A technical co-founder changes the analysis completely, and is the single
  highest-value person you could meet.

**Verdict: eliminated by skills and capital.** Revisit the moment either changes.

## Buying a small business

Often the correct answer at $250k+ of capital: buy $300k of SDE for 3× with an
SBA loan and own a $1M asset on day one. It is the highest-expected-value path
in the whole space.

- SBA acquisition needs **10–15% down** — $50,000–100,000 on a $500k deal.
- Seller financing reduces but does not eliminate this.

**Verdict: eliminated by capital**, and worth remembering. If the recommended
plan works, you reach $250k+ in cash somewhere around month 10 — at which point
*this* becomes your best move for the second million.

## Commission-only enterprise sales

The one genuine contender, and it deserves a fair hearing because it exploits
exactly the asset you named.

- A strong AE at a high-ACV company earns **$400,000–700,000** on target.
- Two years at that rate clears $1M, with essentially zero capital at risk and
  no business to build.
- The catch: it is a **full-time job**, and you told me you have 20 hours a week.
  Commission-only roles at that level do not exist part-time, because the sales
  cycles require presence.
- The second catch: you build no asset, so there is nothing to sell at the end.

**Verdict: eliminated by hours — but it is your best alternative if that
changes.** If you ever free up to full-time and want lower variance than
entrepreneurship, this is the path with the highest floor. It is genuinely a
better risk-adjusted bet than the recommended plan for most people. It just is
not available to you at twenty hours a week.

---

## The pattern

Every rejected option fails on a constraint you actually stated, not on my
taste. Capital eliminates four of them. Hours eliminate the fifth. The deadline
eliminates the sixth. Skills eliminate the seventh.

What survives is the only thing that converts *sales ability* into *an asset*
without capital: sell a service at a price high enough to pay someone else to
deliver it, and build it so that someone else can own it.

That is not a compromise. Given your four constraints, it is the answer.
