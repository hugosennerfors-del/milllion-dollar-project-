# What would prove this wrong, and when

A plan you cannot falsify is a wish. These are the numbers that tell you whether
the thing is working, decided in advance so you cannot move the goalposts later
— which you will want to, around month seven, when the numbers are ambiguous
and quitting feels like failure rather than information.

## The dividing line

I ran the recommended plan 10,000 times and separated the futures that reached
$1M from the ones that did not, then asked what distinguished them and how early.
The answer is that they separate almost immediately:

| Month | Median of runs that **reached $1M** | Median of runs that **did not** | Your line |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 5 clients | 3 clients | **4** |
| 6 | 9 clients | 5 clients | **7** |
| 9 | 12 clients | 7 clients | **9** |
| 12 | 14 clients | 8 clients | **11** |
| 18 | 18 clients | 10 clients | **14** |
| 24 | 20 clients | 11 clients | — |

By **month 6** the two populations barely overlap. This is good news: you do not
have to wait two years to find out. You have to wait six months, and the cost of
finding out is six months of part-time work that teaches you a durable skill
either way.

Cash, for the same plan: **$39k by month 3, $103k by month 6, $309k by month 12,
$581k by month 18.** Those are medians; the 25th percentile runs about 40% lower
and is still on a winning path.

## Checkpoints

**Day 30 — infrastructure works.**
Sending domain live and warmed, 500 named prospects with named humans, 400+
touches sent, a measurable reply rate. *If reply rate is under 0.5%*, you have a
deliverability problem or a list problem, not a market problem. Diagnose before
sending another email — volume on a broken channel just burns the domain.

**Day 60 — the offer converts.**
At least one client signed, and the two-price experiment underway. *If zero
clients after 800 touches and 6+ calls*, the offer is wrong, not the volume.
Change the promise, the niche, or the price — not the activity level. Sending
1,600 bad emails instead of 800 is the most common wrong response here.

**Day 90 — you are not the product.**
2–4 clients, delivery process written down, first contractor hired or being
hired. *If you are still personally delivering everything*, you have built a job
and the ceiling is coming. This is the moment it is cheapest to fix.

**Month 6 — the real checkpoint.** ← *the one that matters*
**7 clients.** This is the strongest single predictor in the whole model.
- **9 or more:** you are on a winning trajectory. Keep going, do not change the plan.
- **5 to 8:** viable but tight. Raise price or improve the funnel; do not add hours.
- **Under 5:** the 24-month version of this plan is probably not going to work.

That last line is not a reason to quit. It is a reason to **switch to the
36-month plan**, which takes the same business from 62% to 82%. You will have
built real revenue, a real skill, and a real asset. Extending the deadline is
the single cheapest thing available to you, and month 6 is when you should
consider spending it.

**Month 12 — is it an asset or a job?**
11+ clients, $120k+/mo revenue, ~$300k cash, and you out of delivery. Test it by
disappearing for three weeks. *If revenue moves while you are gone*, you cannot
sell this, and two-thirds of your odds live in the sale. Fix that before you
chase another client.

**Month 14–16 — start the sale, or commit to 36 months.**
A decision point people miss because the model hides it. Selling a small agency
takes 6–12 months from decision to wire. If you want cash in hand at month 24,
you must start now, at a lower SDE than the month-24 figure. If you would rather
keep compounding, commit to the 36-month plan deliberately, in writing, rather
than by drift.

## When to actually stop

Three conditions. Any one of them, and the correct move is to stop rather than
push harder:

1. **Month 9, fewer than 5 clients, after genuinely testing two offers and two
   price points.** Not one offer tried twice — two real attempts. At that point
   the evidence says this specific market does not want this specific thing from
   you, and more effort buys more of the same information.

2. **Reply rate under 0.5% across three different lists and two different
   domains.** Either the channel is dead in your niche or the offer has no pull.
   Both are real findings. Neither is fixed by volume.

3. **You have stopped doing the work.** Not "you are tired" — tired is normal
   and month seven is always grim. *Stopped.* Three consecutive weeks under 10
   hours means the plan has already ended; the only question is whether you
   admit it and reclaim the time. The model has no parameter for motivation, and
   it is the most common real cause of failure in this plan.

## What you keep either way

Worth stating, because it changes how much risk this actually is. If you run
this for nine months and stop, you do not end at zero. You end with:

- a repeatable outbound system you built and can use on anything,
- probably $50k–150k in cash (the p10 of the recommended plan at month 12 is
  well above zero, and P(under $100k) at 24 months is approximately zero),
- proof you can sell, in the form of clients who paid you,
- a list, a niche, and a reputation in it.

The realistic downside here is not ruin. It is **two years of part-time work
that pays reasonably and does not reach a million dollars.** That is a bad
outcome relative to the goal and a fine one relative to most things people do
with two years of evenings.

Which is, in the end, the honest case for this plan. Not that it will work —
it is 62% — but that the version where it fails is one you can live with.
